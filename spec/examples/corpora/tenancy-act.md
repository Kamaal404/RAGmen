# Residential Tenancy Act (sample)

A short, invented statute used to test onboarding a second corpus type. It is
not real law.

# Contents

- Part 1 - General Provisions
- Part 2 - Deposits
- Part 3 - Repairs and Access

# Part 1 - General Provisions

## Article 1 - Definitions

In this Act, a landlord is the person who grants the right to occupy a dwelling,
and a tenant is the person who holds that right under a tenancy agreement.

## Article 2 - Scope

This Act applies to every tenancy agreement for a dwelling, except holiday lets
of fewer than thirty days.

# Part 2 - Deposits

## Article 3 - Maximum deposit

A landlord must not require a deposit greater than two months of rent. A tenant
may recover any excess from the landlord at any time during the tenancy.

## Article 4 - Return of deposit

The landlord must return the deposit within fourteen days of the end of the
tenancy, less the cost of repairing damage beyond normal wear. See Article 5
for the landlord's own repair duties, which cannot be charged to the tenant.

# Part 3 - Repairs and Access

## Article 5 - Landlord repair duties

The landlord must keep the structure, heating and water supply in repair. When
the tenant reports a defect in writing, the landlord must begin repairs within
seven days.

## Article 6 - Access for repairs

The landlord may enter the dwelling to carry out repairs only after giving the
tenant twenty-four hours of written notice, except in an emergency.
